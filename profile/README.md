# OpenSeed

**Open source has a funding problem that donations alone have never solved: the value flows one way.**

Projects give their work away. Some of that work ends up underneath companies worth billions. Almost none of that success finds its way back to the commons it was built on — not because people are hostile to the idea, but because there is no mechanism. Every attempt so far has depended on goodwill remembering to act.

OpenSeed is an attempt to build the mechanism.

---

## SPARK

**SPARK** — *Scaling Public Alignment with Recursive Kindling* — is a proposed pledge for projects that want to share part of their future success with open source.

Under the current draft, 4% would go to other projects that have taken the pledge, or agree to take it as a condition of support. A further 1% would support upstream dependencies without requiring them to sign. Payments would begin only after a three-year grace period following commercialization.

The design has one property that makes it different from a donation drive: **it is recursive**. Because the 4% may only fund projects that have taken the pledge themselves, each generation of funded projects becomes the next generation of funders.

The closest analogy is not a tax. It is **GPL, applied to money instead of code**:

> Nobody is required to open their code. But if you distribute it, you must. Under SPARK: if you never capture value, you owe nothing. If you do, you pay it forward.

These percentages and the grace period are proposed terms, not settled requirements. The full pledge text and a plain-language explanation of how "captured value" is calculated will be published before any project is asked to sign.

---

## What OpenSeed is

SPARK is a mechanism. **OpenSeed is the attempt to make it actually run.**

A pledge framework on its own has a gap in the middle: someone has to hold the agreements, verify that a project which said it would pay forward actually did, and absorb the cost of doing that so individual projects don't have to. OpenSeed is being built to be that institution.

The reference point we keep coming back to is the **Software Freedom Law Center** in its early years — it hired people, took donations, paid salaries, and defended the GPL until the GPL became something nobody needed defending. If SPARK works, the institution behind it should eventually have nothing left to do.

### Where we are now

OpenSeed has initial funding for a first pilot, but we have not selected a partner project. The pledge text is still being prepared for publication, so we are not asking projects to sign it yet.

Our immediate goal is to find a project willing to examine the proposed terms with us, identify where they do not work, and help design a pilot that can be evaluated in public.

This is early. It is closer to a startup than to a foundation, and it may not work. We think the odds are good enough to be worth running the experiment in public.

## How to get involved

- **Maintain a project that might fit the first pilot?** [Contact Richard](#contact) with a link to the project, who maintains it, and how you expect it might capture value. An introduction is a conversation, not a commitment to sign.
- **Know a promising project?** Send us its name and why you think it fits the [criteria for a first partner](https://github.com/openseed-cc/spark-pledge/blob/main/FAQ.md#isnt-the-real-bottleneck-picking-the-first-project-not-the-mechanism).
- **See a flaw in the mechanism?** [Open an issue](https://github.com/openseed-cc/spark-pledge/issues). Please point to the assumption or term that fails and, if possible, give a concrete example.
- **Have legal, accounting, or auditing experience?** We especially want feedback on how obligations could be explained, reported, and verified in practice.

[Contact Richard](#contact) · [Open an issue](https://github.com/openseed-cc/spark-pledge/issues) · [Read the FAQ](https://github.com/openseed-cc/spark-pledge/blob/main/FAQ.md)

There are a few co-founding partners working on this who are not public yet. Everything here is being built in the open as it is decided.
